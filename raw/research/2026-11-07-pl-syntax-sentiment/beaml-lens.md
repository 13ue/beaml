# The BEAML lens

**Status:** Temporary research KB, 2026-11-07, not ingested into `wiki/`, not linked from `wiki/index.md`. Ingest decision pending OWNER.

Claim tags: **[Verified]** (an input page states it with its source), **[Inference]** (the synthesizer's reasoning), **[Proposal]** (a suggestion, not a decision), **[Unknown]** (not established). Every alignment judgment in this page is **[Inference]**.

Shorthands: `L n` = row n of the [loved table](loved.md#1-ranked-table-top-celebrated-features); `H n` = row n of the [hated table](hated.md#ranked-table-the-top-26); `P` = the [BEAML feature checklist](beaml-syntax-profile.md#5-feature-checklist); `DL-nnn` = an entry in the [decision log](../../../wiki/planning/decision-log.md); `D0nn` = an open item in [decisions to be made](../../../wiki/planning/decisions-to-be-made.md); `Q` = the profile's [open syntax questions](beaml-syntax-profile.md#4-open-syntax-questions).

## 1. Method

Each row of the loved and hated tables was checked against the BEAML feature checklist, the design intents and the decision table in [beaml-syntax-profile.md](beaml-syntax-profile.md); rows with no bearing on BEAML (borrow checker, Bash quoting, Perl sigils, Lisp parentheses, C declaration syntax, Haskell `String`) were dropped or folded into a neighbour. The BEAML status column repeats the profile's status words (present / planned / rejected / open / not addressed) and its tags; nothing about BEAML in this page comes from outside the profile and the pages it links.

## 3. Where BEAML is already aligned with what people love

- `enum` with payload variants and keep-value variant (DL-061, DL-062, DL-082): loved rows 1 and 2, the most-cited cluster in the evidence; its absence in Go is hated row 21.
- `-> T ! E` with the pipe stopping on failure (DL-015, DL-093): loved row 3 (errors as values with `?`-style propagation) and the avoidance of hated row 3 (`if err != nil` per call).
- `|>` in any expression with constructors as steps (DL-014, DL-086): loved row 5, the single most-upvoted favourite-feature post, and the "left to right" intent is recorded in DL-086.
- One expression per body, no assignment, no statements ([P](beaml-syntax-profile.md#5-feature-checklist)): loved rows 8 and 9, and hated row 24 (`if (a = b)`) is unwritable.
- `type` derives decode, structs, spans and replay (DL-126): loved row 15 (data classes, derive) and the answer to hated row 10 (ceremony).
- No casts, no `any`, no guessing fallback, ENUM LITERALS bind 1:1 (DL-028, DL-082, [vertical-integration](../../../wiki/concepts/vertical-integration.md)): the inverse of hated rows 2, 9 and 18 (coercion, sentinels, implicit typing).
- Macros rejected, "never magic", escape hatch per declaration (DL-126): loved row 18 (no hidden control flow) and the inverse of hated rows 20 and 26.
- `beaml fmt` with column alignment and mark normalization (DL-083, DL-080): loved row 23 (gofmt stance).
- Hard errors with help lines for retired spellings (DL-074): the small-scale start of loved row 6 (compiler as colleague).
- `migrate` keeps history in source (DL-022, DL-071): the data-level counterpart of hated row 23 (breaking transitions) and loved row 22 (compatibility).

## 4. Where BEAML risks what people hate

- Null and absence (hated row 1, loved row 4): the profile marks `Option` / optional field as not addressed [Unknown]. BEAML has no null, so the hated row does not apply as written, but a program that must say "absent" has no form and no open decision item. The historical spec S06 required the distinction; nothing since records it.
- `-> T ! E1 | E2` and checked exceptions (hated row 11): the declared failure list is structurally Java's `throws`. What distinguishes it: failures are values in the pipe, not invisible jumps (Spolsky's objection falls away), and totality forbids catching and swallowing. What does not distinguish it: Hejlsberg's versionability problem, a new failure on a deep effect ripples through every `!` list above it, and the profile has no page weighing that. Generic `-> T ! E` (DL-093 "for later") is the usual escape and is not built.
- Complexity creep (hated row 4): BEAML's novel forms (`migrate`, `retire`, `redacted`, `with` release, VERSION MARK, FATE OPERATIONS, ENTRY LINE, policy on `effect` lines) are each decided singly. The evidence says that is exactly how C++ and Rust got their complaint; the profile records no budget or count. [Inference]
- String interpolation rejected (loved row 16): `+` concatenation is the only way to build a String, and `+` is the only operator in the grammar. The reason ("must stay data") is inferred from a fixture comment, not recorded as a decision with its trade-off.
- Minimal inference and `-> Type` on every clause (loved row 14, hated row 10): explicitness is intended (DL-083 "clauses read as a table"), but the evidence lists "writing every type" as the cost people avoid. Agents may not mind; the surveys are of humans. [Inference]
- Optional separators (hated row 25): newline-or-comma between fields and variants, bare `Pending` next to `Approved()`, with no page analysing ambiguous juxtapositions. [Unknown] whether a silent misparse exists; the class of bug is the one H25 names.
- Pipe debuggability and error branching (loved theme 2.4 objections): BEAML's answer is `with ... do ... else ... end`, decided (DL-014) but grammar only; until it compiles, a pipe that must branch on a failure has no form. [Verified in profile 1.11]
- Compiler error quality (loved row 6): intent is "Rust-level"; the evidence says rustc's errors are a decade of sustained work by hundreds of contributors. Diagnostics beyond two help lines are [Unknown].
- Concurrency and coloring (loved row 13, hated row 5): BEAML has no concurrency syntax; the pure/effectful split via `!` and `effect` is a deliberate two-color system (DL-120 forbids a constructor reaching an EFFECT). The evidence hates coloring when it infects callers; BEAML's `!` does propagate to callers. Whether that reads as "coloring" to users is [Unknown].
- `migrate`, `redacted`, `effect` transports, FATE OPERATIONS, ENTRY LINE: no industry analogue in either table [Unknown]. They are BEAML's distinguishing surface and the one area where this evidence says nothing either way.

## 5. Gaps in BEAML's syntax surface vs top-10 loved features

Loved features the profile marks [Unknown] or not addressed, one line each.

- Null / Option handling (loved 4): not addressed; no `Option`, `nil` or optional field ([P](beaml-syntax-profile.md#5-feature-checklist)).
- Closures / lambdas (loved 10): not addressed; functions exist only as top-level clauses [Unknown].
- Comprehensions and list literals (loved 11): no list TYPE or literal; DL-129 already needs a `List` TYPE.
- Map literals (loved theme 2.8 context): no map TYPE; records lower to Elixir maps at the host only ([P](beaml-syntax-profile.md#5-feature-checklist)).
- String interpolation (loved 16): rejected for now; `#{...}` is literal text.
- Lightweight concurrency form (loved 13): no spawn, receive or task form on a runtime built for it.
- Imports and modules (no loved row; listed by the profile): `import` is a reserved word only; module form is roadmap part 4 without an option table ([P](beaml-syntax-profile.md#5-feature-checklist), [Q](beaml-syntax-profile.md#4-open-syntax-questions)).
- Booleans, floats, negative literals, arithmetic (no loved row; profile gaps): `Bool` and `Float` absent, `+` is concatenation only, division decided and unbuilt (DL-030) ([profile 6](beaml-syntax-profile.md#6-gaps-noticed-in-the-kb-for-the-synthesis-worker)).
- Traits / protocols (loved 21): `interface` reserved, no form, no decision item.
- Async form: n/a as a gap in the hated sense; see concurrency above. The evidence prefers no `async` keyword at all (loved 13, hated 5).

## 6. Contested features and BEAML's current position

Evidence from [loved section 3](loved.md#3-contested-features-loved-by-some-hated-by-others) and [hated theme 7](hated.md#7-whitespace-and-punctuation-wars); BEAML position from the profile.

- **Significant whitespace.** Praised as enforced readability, attacked as "detrimental cleverness" and invisible structure [Verified both]. BEAML: braces, newline or comma as separator, `do ... end` for `with`; whitespace not significant. The Go/Rust/Zig/Gleam side.
- **Macros / metaprogramming.** "I can't go without now" vs Gleam's no-plan and Go's "Reflection is never clear" [Verified]. BEAML: rejected by principle; the compiler derives (DL-126). The Go/Gleam side.
- **Operator overloading.** Kotlin offers it, Zig forbids it to keep `a + b` honest [Verified]. BEAML: not addressed [Unknown]; the only operator is `+` on Strings, so the question has not arisen. De facto the Zig side, by omission [Inference].
- **Extent of type inference.** "Looks dynamic, still statically typed" vs harder-to-read full inference [Verified]. BEAML: minimal; every clause declares its return TYPE, heads need `: Type` unless a pattern fixes it. The explicit end.
- **Implicit returns / everything is an expression.** Loved in Rust, Ruby, Kotlin `when`; Zig and Go prefer explicit intent [Verified]. BEAML: a body is one expression, its value is the result; no `return`. The Rust/Ruby side.
- **Pipelines everywhere.** Beloved, misused, harder to debug and to branch on errors [Verified]. BEAML: `|>` is the central expression form (DL-086), including in argument and field position (D6B-25); the branching answer (`with ... else`) is grammar only.
- **Formatter-enforced layout.** "No one's favorite, yet everyone's favorite" [Verified]. BEAML: `beaml fmt` aligns clause columns and effect lines and normalizes VERSION MARKS (DL-013, DL-080, DL-083); partly built. The gofmt side, with semantic rules added.
- **Dynamic typing** (also contested): third most-liked Python feature vs top missing JS feature [Verified]. BEAML: static only, no `any`. Not contested inside BEAML.
- **Async/await vs green threads**: BEAML has neither form [Unknown].

## 7. Where the evidence is silent

The tables say nothing about schema history in source, PII markers in types, declared transports, inbound entry declarations or retention lines. BEAML's most distinctive forms therefore get no signal from this research; a different evidence base (schema-evolution tooling, data-privacy engineering, effect-system languages such as Koka or Unison) would be needed [Unknown].

## 8. Candidate open decisions

[Proposal] Questions only, for the OWNER to accept or drop into [decisions-to-be-made.md](../../../wiki/planning/decisions-to-be-made.md). No recommendation is given.

1. Does BEAML need an absent-value form (an `Option`-like TYPE, an optional field, or a documented `enum` idiom), and how does it meet the historical S06 "missing vs explicit null" requirement? ## 9. Limits

- The evidence is industry sentiment about other languages, not BEAML user data; BEAML has no users yet, so nothing here measures how BEAML's forms are received.
- Every alignment judgment and every "mirror" pairing is the synthesizer's inference; the input pages do not pair rows.
- The BEAML side rests on one profile page dated 2026-11-07 and the pages it cites; forms marked grammar-only or sketch may change before they compile.
- The rankings in both tables are researcher aggregations with the limits listed in [README.md](README.md#evidence-limits); a row's number is not a measured frequency.
- The evidence base is biased toward general-purpose languages; BEAML's distinctive forms (schema history, PII, effects) get no signal, see section 7.
